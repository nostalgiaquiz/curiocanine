## How the program works

### A reward-based approach

The official page says every part of the course uses force-free methods. In practice, positive reinforcement dog training means rewarding the behaviors you like, often with small food treats, and making it easy for the dog to succeed, without fear, pain or intimidation. Professional veterinary behavior organizations, including the American Veterinary Society of Animal Behavior, generally recommend reward-based methods over aversive ones, so the stated philosophy is in line with mainstream professional guidance.

### Mental enrichment through games

The distinctive part is the format. Instead of drilling commands, the course uses games such as hide and seek, treasure hunts and puzzle-style activities. The idea behind **dog brain training** is that mental effort can tire and satisfy a dog in a way physical exercise alone may not, while building focus and impulse control. To try this style before spending anything, our [Brain Games](/brain-games/) and [Dog Enrichment](/dog-enrichment/) sections are free starting points.

### The "hidden intelligence" claim

The sales page explains its method through the idea of neuroplasticity, arguing that mental stimulation makes a dog's brain more receptive to learning and that problem behaviors then fade. It presents this as simple science.

**Our observation:** the page does not cite specific studies for this explanation, so we cannot assess it. Enrichment and training can certainly help a bored dog, but "more intelligent dogs are easier to train" is a broad statement, and the real-world result will depend on the individual dog, its history and its environment.

## Course structure: the seven modules

The official page organizes the training into seven modules with a school theme, which you could think of as seven learning levels. Each module lists example games. This is the seller's description:

1. **Preschool** covers foundations: attention and eye contact, target training, and a simple attention game called the airplane game.
2. **Elementary School** uses the dog's senses, with games including a treasure hunt, a muffin tin game and a ball pit game.
3. **High School** focuses on patience and impulse control, with games such as "jazz up and settle down," a bottle game and bobbing for treats.
4. **College** works on concentration and motor skills through the shell game, an "open sesame" game about staying calm around an open door, and a magic carpet game.
5. **University** builds on impulse control and confidence, with hide and seek, a "look at that" game for dogs that bark at passersby, and a hot and cold game.
6. **Graduation** introduces more advanced skills, including leg weaving, serpentine and spiral movements, and a name recognition game where a dog learns to pick out toys by name.
7. **Einstein** is the showpiece level, with trick-style games such as tidying up toys, stacking rings and playing a toy piano.

The page says there are more than 21 games in total.

**Our observation:** the progression looks logical, moving from attention and foundations toward impulse control and then more complex tasks. Games such as muffin tin puzzles, hide and seek and treasure hunts are common enrichment ideas, so a paid course may mainly add sequencing and video demonstrations. For a beginner, that structure is useful, though not unique.

## Potential benefits

Without promising any outcome, these are the benefits that a structured, game-based, reward-based course might reasonably offer:

- **Structure for beginners.** A clear sequence can help owners who do not know what to teach first.
- **Mental stimulation.** Games can give an under-stimulated dog an outlet, especially when long walks are not possible.
- **Better communication.** Regular short sessions can help owners learn to mark and reward good behavior consistently.
- **Impulse control practice.** Some modules focus on waiting and settling, which are useful everyday skills.
- **One place for common topics.** The archive and bonus course may help with issues such as jumping or chewing.

These benefits depend on how the course is used, and none is guaranteed.

## Limitations and disadvantages

**The sales page makes very strong claims.** It says almost any behavior problem can be quickly and easily solved and describes the system as proven. **Our observation:** no course can promise that for every dog. Results vary with the dog's age, breed, history, health and environment, and with how consistently the owner practices.

**Owner consistency is essential.** Progress depends on regular practice, and it is likely to stall if sessions are skipped.

**It is not individualized.** An online course cannot watch your dog or spot subtle body language the way a trainer can. A support system is mentioned, but we have not tested it.

**It is not a substitute for professional help in serious cases.** The page lists problems such as reactivity, anxiety and fear. These can have medical or emotional causes. If your dog shows aggression, bites, severe fear or sudden behavior changes, please speak with a veterinarian first and consider a qualified behavior professional.

**Urgency language.** The page uses limited-availability wording and a "launch price" framing. Take the time to decide.

## Our verdict

Brain Training for Dogs looks like a reasonably structured, reward-based course built around games, with a clear progression across its seven modules and a number of extras. For a beginner who wants a guided plan for enrichment and basic training, that could be useful, and the stated approach matches mainstream positive reinforcement thinking.

Our main reservation is how the course is promoted. The sales page makes sweeping promises that we cannot verify. A realistic expectation is a structured set of games and lessons that can help if you practice consistently, not a quick fix for serious behavior problems.

If your goals are enrichment, focus and everyday manners, it may be worth considering, and the refund guarantee should be checked carefully. If you are dealing with aggression, severe anxiety or health issues, start with your veterinarian and a qualified professional. If you are unsure, try some free games first.
